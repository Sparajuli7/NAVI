# 07 — Avenue: NAVI as a "Settling-In" Guide for International Students

A focused research avenue: NAVI as a **personal language + settling-in companion
for newly-arrived international students** who must learn the host language to
*live* (errands, housing, healthcare, campus, friendships) — not just to pass a
class. This is arguably NAVI's single strongest niche: a well-defined, high-need,
easily-recruited population (they're on your campus), a real problem with public-
health stakes, and a near-perfect fit to NAVI's differentiators.

> TL;DR recommended path: **(1) a fast formative needs-finding study** (interviews
> + diary) → **(2) build a "Newcomer Guide" mode** → **(3) a longitudinal field
> deployment across the arrival period** measuring acculturative stress, real-world
> host-language use, and social integration. Phase 1 is publishable on its own and
> de-risks the flagship Phase 3.

---

## 1. Why this niche is strong

- **The problem is real and measured.** Language barriers are repeatedly found to
  be the **most significant stressor** for international students — above academic
  demands — and are tied to acculturative stress, mental health, and social
  isolation (see refs below). ~1/3 of international students report significant L2
  anxiety.
- **Language proficiency is the *moderator* of successful adjustment.** Host-
  language skill predicts less acculturative stress and better sociocultural
  adaptation; "integration" (vs assimilation/separation/marginalization) is the
  most adaptive acculturation strategy — and it *requires* host-language use.
- **The field is open.** CALL/MALL research for migrants/newcomers is explicitly
  scarce (a 2025 *ReCALL* special issue exists precisely to address the gap).
- **Perfect fit to NAVI's differentiators:**
  - *Location/dialect awareness* → teaches how locals *actually* speak where the
    student now lives (not textbook).
  - *Camera-OCR "point at the world"* → decode real signage, forms, menus,
    transit, prescriptions — the newcomer's daily friction points.
  - *Companion + memory + warmth* → emotional support for acculturative stress;
    remembers the student's situation across the hardest first weeks.
  - *Calibrated/adaptive code-switching* → "survival-level" beginners get L1
    scaffolding that fades (ties directly to Avenue #1 / `04_...md`).
  - *Spaced repetition + scenarios* → the recurring real errands become the
    curriculum.
- **Recruitable + fundable.** International-student offices, ESL/EAP programs, and
  student-wellbeing units are natural partners; wellbeing + integration outcomes
  attract education, public-health, and HCI-for-good funding.

## 2. Theoretical spine (what makes it a *paper*, not a feature)

Anchor the work in three literatures so reviewers see rigor:

1. **Acculturation theory** (Berry): integration as adaptive; host-language use as
   the mechanism. NAVI is an *integration-supporting* intervention.
2. **Willingness-to-Communicate & FL anxiety** (MacIntyre; Horwitz): a low-stakes
   companion lowers anxiety and raises real-world WTC — the bridge from app to
   street.
3. **Situated learning + interactional/pragmatic competence + social-network SLA**
   (study-abroad research; INoLP): competence grows through *everyday situated
   interaction* and *expanding L2 social ties*. NAVI's job is to **scaffold and
   push learners into real-world interaction**, not replace it.

**Positioning sentence:** *NAVI is a situated, location-aware LLM companion that
scaffolds newcomers' real-world host-language use during the acculturation window,
aiming to reduce acculturative stress and accelerate integration.*

---

## 3. The gap NAVI fills

Existing tools split into: (a) generic MALL (Duolingo — decontextualized, not
newcomer-specific), (b) translation tools (solve the moment, don't build
competence or confidence), (c) classroom EAP (not situated in daily life), and (d)
early migrant-MALL projects (mostly pre-LLM, content-delivery, not conversational/
companion). **No LLM companion is designed as a situated settling-in guide that
bridges classroom → real-world use while supporting the emotional side of
relocation.** That's the flag.

---

## 4. Research questions (across the phased path)

**Formative (Phase 1):**
- RQ0: What are newly-arrived international students' actual host-language needs,
  breakdowns, and coping strategies in their first weeks, and where do current
  tools fail? (→ design requirements)

**Flagship (Phase 3):**
- RQ1 (stress/wellbeing): Does using NAVI's Newcomer Guide reduce **acculturative
  stress** (ASSIS) over the arrival period vs a control (e.g., generic app /
  waitlist)?
- RQ2 (real-world use): Does it increase self-reported + logged **real-world host-
  language interactions** and **willingness to communicate**?
- RQ3 (competence): Does it improve **survival-pragmatic competence** (situationally
  appropriate language for real tasks) and functional vocabulary retention?
- RQ4 (integration/social ties): Does it correlate with **sociocultural adaptation**
  and growth in L2 social-network ties (INoLP-style)?
- RQ5 (mechanism/design): Which NAVI features (situated camera tasks, companion
  callbacks, code-switching, scenario rehearsal) drive the effects? (mediation +
  telemetry)

## 5. Recommended path (phased) — the "best path of research"

### Phase 1 — Formative needs-finding study *(do this first; ~4–6 weeks; publishable)*
- **Method:** semi-structured interviews (n≈12–20 newly-arrived international
  students) + a 1–2 week **experience-sampling / diary** of real language
  breakdowns ("moment it went wrong today"). Optionally shadow a few errands.
- **Output:** a taxonomy of newcomer language needs + breakdown moments + design
  requirements. **This is a standalone qualitative HCI/CALL paper** *and* the
  spec for Phase 2. Low risk, fast, IRB-light (interviews).
- **Analysis:** reflexive thematic analysis; affinity diagramming.

### Phase 2 — Design & build the "Newcomer Guide" mode *(build; ~4–8 weeks)*
Turn Phase 1 findings into a NAVI mode:
- **Survival-first curriculum:** scenario packs for the newcomer's real errands
  (SIM card, bank, lease, clinic, campus registration, groceries, transit).
- **Situated camera tasks:** "show me this form/sign/menu" → decode + teach the 3
  phrases you need to act on it.
- **Just-in-time + spaced follow-up:** capture the real situation, then space-
  repeat the language it required.
- **Acculturation-aware companion:** check-ins, normalizing stress, nudging one
  small real-world interaction per day ("micro-mission: order in {L2} today").
- **Adaptive code-switching** tuned to survival-beginner (heavy L1 → fading).

### Phase 3 — Longitudinal field deployment *(flagship; spans the arrival window, ~6–12 weeks)*
- **Design:** ideally a **randomized field experiment** — NAVI Newcomer Guide vs
  active control (generic language app) — with newly-arrived students, run across
  the first weeks in-country (the highest-stress, highest-plasticity window). If
  randomization isn't feasible, a within-subjects/pre-post with a matched
  comparison + rich telemetry.
- **Timing matters:** recruit *at or before arrival/orientation*; measure the
  arc, not a snapshot.
- **Measures (triangulated):**
  - *Stress/wellbeing:* **ASSIS** (Acculturative Stress Scale for International
    Students), a wellbeing scale, FL anxiety (FLCAS short).
  - *Language use/affect:* WTC; weekly experience-sampling of real interactions;
    NAVI telemetry (see `05_...md`: `research_events` — sessions, situated camera
    tasks, user L2-token production, micro-missions completed).
  - *Competence:* survival-pragmatic scenario assessment (role-play / DCT-style)
    + functional-vocabulary retention.
  - *Integration/social:* sociocultural adaptation scale; INoLP-style L2 social-
    network mapping (size, tie strength, L2-speaking contacts) at start vs end.
  - *Qualitative:* biweekly check-ins + exit interviews (the story behind the
    numbers; drives UI refinement).
- **Analysis:** mixed-effects / growth-curve models over time × condition;
  mediation (does reduced anxiety / increased real-world use explain outcomes?);
  thematic analysis of interviews.

**Why this order is "best":** Phase 1 gives you a paper and a validated design
spec quickly and cheaply; Phase 2 builds the right thing (not a guess); Phase 3 is
the high-impact flagship. Each phase de-risks the next, and every phase yields a
publishable output.

## 6. Population-specific ethics & recruitment (important)

- **International students can be a vulnerable population** (visa precarity, power
  imbalance, possible distress). Your IRB will likely scrutinize this — build in:
  clear voluntariness (no bearing on enrollment/visa), a distress-referral path
  (link to campus counseling), data protection for sensitive disclosures, and
  culturally/linguistically accessible consent (offer consent in the L1).
- **Recruit via** the international-student office, EAP/ESL programs, orientation,
  and student associations. Partnering with these offices also aids retention and
  legitimacy.
- **Avoid coercion:** compensation approved by IRB; make clear NAVI is not a
  clinical or immigration-advice tool.
- **Longitudinal attrition** is the main risk — schedule reminders, small
  milestone incentives, and keep instruments short.

See `05_instrumentation_data_ethics.md` for the shared logging/consent machinery
(the same `research_events` schema covers this avenue).
