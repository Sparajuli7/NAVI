# 08 — Phase 1: Interview Guide, Diary Prompts & Scenario Selection

For the **formative needs-finding study** in Avenue 07 (NAVI as a settling-in guide
for international students). Goal: learn (a) *what* newcomers actually struggle
with, (b) *which real-life scenarios* matter most, and (c) *how they cope today* —
then turn that into design requirements + a prioritized scenario roadmap for
building. Post-IRB, this is close to run-ready; adapt `‹…›` to your context.

Two data streams:
1. **Semi-structured interviews** (n≈12–20) — depth, stories, requirements.
2. **1–2 week diary / experience-sampling** — real breakdowns in the moment.

Method note: use the **Critical Incident Technique** (ask for concrete past
events, not opinions) and keep questions **open and non-leading**. Save NAVI-
specific probes for the *end* so you don't bias needs toward our features.

---

## Part A — Interview guide

### A0. Warm-up & background (5 min)
- Where did you move from, and when did you arrive? What are you here to do?
- What language(s) do you speak? How would you rate your ‹host-language› right now?
- Before arriving, how did you prepare for the language? Did it match reality?

### A1. The first weeks — critical incidents (core; 15 min)
*The richest data. Push for specifics.*
- Walk me through your **first week**. What did you have to do that involved the
  language? Take me hour by hour if you can.
- Tell me about a specific time in your first weeks when the **language got in the
  way**. What happened, what did you need to do, how did it end?
- Tell me about a time you **avoided** doing something because of the language.
  What was it? What did you do instead?
- When was the last time you felt **stuck or embarrassed** because of language?
- Was there a moment you felt **proud** — something you pulled off in the language?
- What's the thing you were **most anxious about** before it happened? Did it turn
  out as bad as you feared?

### A2. Scenario elicitation & prioritization (core; 15 min)
*This section produces your scenario roadmap. See Part C for the card-sort task.*
- If you list the **situations** where you *must* use the language to get by, what
  are they? (Let them free-list first; don't prompt with our list.)
- Which of those happen **most often**? Which are **rare but really matter**?
- Which are the **scariest / highest-stakes** (where getting it wrong has real
  consequences)?
- Which felt **impossible at first** but got easier? What made it easier?
- Is there a situation you **still can't handle** and just avoid or outsource?
- For a situation you named: what exactly do you need to *say*, *understand*, or
  *read*? Where does it break — speaking, listening, reading a sign/form?
- *(Then run the Part C card sort to rank them.)*

### A3. Current tools & workarounds (10 min)
- What do you use today when you hit a language problem? (Let them list.)
- When do you reach for **Google Translate / a translation app**? What does it do
  well, and where does it fail you?
- Do you use **Duolingo/Babbel/etc.**? Does it help with real daily life here? Why
  / why not?
- Who do you ask for help — friends, family back home, a partner, staff? How does
  that feel (relief? burden? dependence?)?
- What would you *love* a tool to do that nothing does today?

### A4. Emotional & social dimension (10 min)
- How has the language situation affected your **stress / mood / confidence**?
- Are there people you *want* to talk to but don't, because of language?
- How's making **friends / joining things** going? What role does language play?
- Have you had an experience where language made you feel **excluded or judged**?
  *(Handle sensitively; have the counseling-referral info ready — see `05`/`07`.)*
- What would help you feel **braver** about using the language in real life?

### A5. NAVI-specific probes (LAST; 5–10 min — don't lead earlier)
*Show a quick concept or 2–3 screens; get reactions, not validation.*
- If a companion could help with the situations you named, what would make it
  actually useful vs. just another app you delete?
- Reaction to: **pointing your camera at a form/sign/menu** and it explains + gives
  you the phrases to act? Where would you use it?
- Reaction to a companion that **remembers your situation** and checks in / nudges
  one small real-world attempt a day? Helpful or annoying?
- Reaction to it speaking **mostly your language at first** with local phrases mixed
  in, shifting to more of the local language as you improve?
- Would you *trust* it? What would make you not trust it (privacy, wrong info)?

### A6. Wrap-up / co-design (5 min)
- If you could design the "first-month language survival kit," what's in it?
- What's the #1 thing that would have made your arrival easier?
- Anything I should have asked but didn't?

## Part B — Diary / experience-sampling prompts

Delivered 1–3×/day for 1–2 weeks (push notification / short form). Keep each entry
< 60 seconds. This captures reality the interview can't recall.

**Daily "language moment" (main prompt):**
1. Did you use or need ‹host-language› today? Where? *(tap: housing / bank / health
   / transit / shopping / campus / social / official / work / other)*
2. What did you need to do in that moment? *(one line)*
3. How did it go? *(1 = total breakdown … 5 = smooth)*
4. What did you do when it was hard? *(spoke L1 / translation app / asked someone /
   gestured / avoided / gave up / other)*
5. How did it make you feel? *(stressed / embarrassed / fine / proud / other)*
6. *(Optional)* Snap a photo of anything you couldn't read *(sign/form/label)*.

**Weekly reflection:**
- What was the hardest language situation this week?
- What's coming up next week you're dreading (language-wise)?

*(The photo + category data is gold for scenario prioritization AND validates the
camera-OCR feature demand.)*

## Part C — Scenario prioritization method (run inside the interview)

Don't just ask "what matters" — **make them rank**. Two lightweight tasks:

### C1. Card sort
Bring ~20 scenario cards (Part D list) + blanks. Ask them to:
1. Add any missing scenarios on blank cards.
2. Sort into **"had to do this" vs "didn't."**
3. Rank the "had to do" pile by **how hard** it was.
4. Separately, mark the **top 3 scariest / highest-stakes**.

### C2. Score each named scenario on 4 axes (1–5)
Capture per scenario so you can prioritize quantitatively across participants:

| Axis | Question |
|------|----------|
| **Frequency** | How often does this happen? |
| **Difficulty** | How linguistically hard is it? |
| **Stakes** | How bad is it if it goes wrong? |
| **Anxiety** | How stressful does it feel? |

**Build-priority score (suggested):** rank by **Frequency × Stakes** for the core
survival packs, and flag **high-Anxiety** items for the companion/emotional-support
angle. High-frequency + high-stakes + high-difficulty = build first.

## Part D — Candidate scenario catalog (seed list for research *and* building)

Hypothesized from newcomer/acculturation literature (`07_...md`) — use as the card-
sort deck, but let participants override. Each lists the **language functions** it
demands (this is what NAVI actually has to teach). Camera-OCR-heavy items marked 📷.

### Tier 1 — High-frequency + high-stakes (likely build first)
- **Healthcare** 📷 — register with a clinic/GP, describe symptoms, understand
  instructions, pharmacy, emergencies. *Functions: describing, clarifying,
  understanding rapid speech, reading labels/forms.*
- **Housing** 📷 — viewing/lease, landlord, utilities, repairs, roommates. *Requesting,
  negotiating, complaining, reading contracts.*
- **Campus / academic admin** 📷 — registration, student ID, advisor/office hours,
  understanding lectures, group work, library. *Formal requests, academic register,
  reading official email.*
- **Immigration / official bureaucracy** 📷 — residence permit, city registration,
  tax/ID number, official letters (country-specific). *Formal register, forms, high-
  anxiety comprehension.*
- **Banking & phone/SIM** 📷 — open account, cards, bills, mobile plan. *Transactions,
  numbers, reading statements.*

### Tier 2 — High-frequency, everyday
- **Groceries & shopping** 📷 — markets, prices, returns, dietary/allergy needs.
- **Food & restaurants** 📷 — ordering, customizing, allergies, paying/tipping.
- **Transit & navigation** 📷 — tickets, apps, asking directions, signage, delays.
- **Everyday services** — post office, haircut, deliveries, repairs, appointments.

### Tier 3 — Social, emotional & integration (drives retention + wellbeing)
- **Small talk & rapport** — greetings, weather, "how was your weekend," local slang.
- **Making friends / joining in** — clubs, invitations, group chats, plans.
- **Workplace / part-time job** — interview, coworkers, shift talk, customer service.
- **Difficult / high-stakes social** — disagreeing politely, saying no, complaints.

### Tier 4 — Safety & edge cases (rare, critical)
- **Emergencies** — police, lost/stolen, accidents, urgent help.
- **Discrimination / feeling unsafe** — recognizing, responding, seeking help.
  *(Design + research this with care and referral resources.)*

### Cross-cutting language "functions" NAVI must cover (from the above)
Requesting · clarifying/repair ("could you repeat/slow down?") · understanding fast
speech · reading signage & forms 📷 · transactions & numbers · complaining/
negotiating · politeness & register (formal vs casual) · small talk/rapport ·
apologizing · declining. *Pragmatic appropriateness across these is the real
learning target (ties to the study-abroad pragmatics literature in `07`).*

## Part E — From data to decisions (what Phase 1 must output)

Synthesize interviews + diaries into:
1. **A ranked scenario roadmap** (Frequency × Stakes × Anxiety) → the build order
   for NAVI's Newcomer Guide scenario packs.
2. **A breakdown taxonomy** — *where* language fails (speak / listen / read-sign /
   read-form / rapport) → tells you which NAVI capabilities to strengthen (e.g.,
   heavy read-a-form failures → prioritize camera-OCR).
3. **Coping/workaround map** — what they use now → NAVI's competitive wedge.
4. **Emotional map** — highest-anxiety situations → the companion/check-in features.
5. **Design requirements doc** — the spec for Phase 2 build.

These outputs are also the backbone of the Phase 1 paper (a qualitative needs-
finding contribution) and directly seed the Phase 3 measures in `07_...md`.

## Method reminders
- Pilot the guide on 1–2 people; cut questions that don't land.
- Offer the interview + consent in the participant's **L1** where possible.
- Record + transcribe (with consent); code with reflexive thematic analysis; two
  coders on a subset for reliability.
- Keep the diary ultra-short or you'll lose compliance — the daily prompt is 6 taps.
- Non-leading > comprehensive: a great story beats ten yes/no answers.
